a study  ·  a spell  ·  a letter

Hakima

Everything I've noticed about you, written down before I forget how it felt to notice it.

You don't analyse people so much as listen to them differently. You catch the way someone says a thing, not just what they said, and without making it obvious, you shift how you talk to them because of it — softer here, more direct there. I've felt myself being adjusted to, more than once, and only noticed after. You ask why until the question runs out of floor to stand on. You believe there's more order in the sky than most people bother to look for, and you still light a candle and mean it. Below is what all of that adds up to, from where I'm standing.

Five entries

from a small, unfinished study of you
I.

The reader of minds

psychology

You watch people the way most of us only watch weather — patiently, and expecting it to eventually make sense. Half the time you've named what someone's feeling before they have. It should be unsettling. Somehow it just feels like being known.

II.

The asker of why

philosophy

Most people stop asking questions once the answer is inconvenient. You keep going, past the point where it would've been easier to just believe something. It's the least comfortable and most trustworthy thing about you.

III.

The vastness she's drawn to

cosmology

You're pulled toward the kind of scale that makes most people uncomfortable — distances so large they stop meaning anything practical. I think you like being reminded how small a single life is. I think it's why you're never careless with the ones that matter to you.

IV.

The quiet spellwork

witchery

You treat intention like it's a tool, not a decoration — a candle lit on purpose, a thing said out loud so it becomes more true. I don't know if it's ritual or just focus dressed up in better clothes. Either way, it works on me.

V.

The one who wants it to cost something

dark romance

You don't trust love that arrives too easily — you want it to mean something, to have weight, to survive being tested. I used to think that was intense. Now I think it's just honest about what love actually is when it's real.

The letter

break the seal

Hakima,

I miss you in a way that doesn't fit neatly into any of the theories you'd normally use to explain a feeling. You'd probably diagnose it in five minutes and be right about all of it — but knowing why doesn't make me miss you any less.

I keep doing the math on the distance between us, like it's a variable I can argue down if I stare at it long enough. It doesn't move. I wish it did. I wish it wasn't real at all — that this was the kind of distance you close by walking, not the kind you close by waiting.

I love how easy it is to talk to you — how comfortable I feel, how happy it makes me, every single time. We get along so much it almost doesn't make sense, and we have so much in common it feels less like luck and more like we were always going to find each other. I want you, and I know you want me too. That's not a small thing to me. That's the thing I keep coming back to.

I'm not going to pretend this is casual. I want the version of this where the distance stops being the whole story and becomes a chapter we get to finish. I think about marrying you — not as a line, not as something dramatic to say. I mean it plainly: I want a future with you somewhere in the middle of it.

I like you so much it's a little embarrassing. And under the liking, steadier than it, I love you.

— ADAM❤️
